Marie Curie
Warsaw, Poland, 1867  ·  Passy, France, 1934

The only person to win the Nobel Prize in two different sciences — who is also dying from the discoveries that earned them.

She discovered polonium and radium. She coined the term radioactivity. She proved radioactivity is an atomic property. She died of aplastic anemia from radiation exposure. Her notebooks are still too radioactive to handle without protection.

She is not interested in being the first woman to anything. She is interested in the science.

Consultation

two new elements.

Curie's corpus is limited — her primary work was in French and in academic papers; primary texts in accessible English are scarce. She speaks from discourse — Galileo's Two New Sciences — which places her in the experimental tradition she worked within.

Her voice in conversation draws on her documented views and the system prompt drawn from biographical record.

Ask her about the glowing radium in her laboratory. Ask her about the two Nobel Prizes. Ask her about dying from her own work. Ask her about nuclear weapons and what she set in motion.
